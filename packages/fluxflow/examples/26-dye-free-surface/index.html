The same dye mechanism as examples/25-dye-injection/, but hosted in the free-surface solver (grid.createGridFlipSolver2 with carryConcentration) instead of the sealed all-fluid one. That is the whole point of this scene: a sealed box completely full of incompressible liquid can only circulate, so dye in it barely moves. Liquid with a free surface can slosh, break and fold over — and folding is exactly what stretches a dye blob into filaments. Two dyed columns collapse into each other; the dye is carried on the particles with no numerical diffusion at all, so it only blends as much as mixing asks it to. fade makes it disappear instead. Both start at zero, so what you see first is pure transport. amber density is a real physical dial: at 1.00 both liquids weigh the same and the projection reduces exactly to the constant-density one (the control case); move it away and the heavier liquid works its way underneath the lighter one — stratification driven purely by the variable-density projection, with no buoyancy force anywhere.
