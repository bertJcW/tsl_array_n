A dyed drop entering a pool of clear liquid — the classic drop-into-a-liquid experiment, and the reason it is worth having alongside the dam break in examples/26-dye-free-surface/: a dam break is over in about a second and the dye ends as one flat layer, while this keeps producing structure. Three phases, all of them recognisable from the real thing: an impact crater with its wall lined in dye, a Worthington jet thrown back up as the crater rebounds, and then the dyed mass rolling into a descending mushroom. The shape comes from the impact — the drop deposits vorticity and the vorticity rolls the dye up. The slow descent afterwards comes from the density difference alone, acting through the pressure projection; there is no buoyancy force in the solver. Set the dye density to 1.00 and the crater and jet are unchanged while the descent stops, which is the control case.
